\section{Identification}
\label{sec:identification}

Let $\ell$ denote location, $x$ the structured attributes, and $W$ the text
embedding, and let the scalar treatment $T$ be a fixed linear summary of $W$, the
oriented leading pooled principal component. Write $L=L(\ell)$ for a basis of
functions of location used as controls. We take the outcome to follow the
structural model
\begin{equation}
Y=\theta\,T+\beta\,U+f(x)+\varepsilon,
\label{eq:struct}
\end{equation}
in which $U$ is an unobserved, spatially patterned driver of price.

\begin{assumption}[Spatial confounder]\label{as:id-U}
The unobserved confounder is location-measurable, $U=h(\ell)$ for a measurable $h$.
This says the confounding travels entirely through geography, the case the
spatial-confounding literature is built for; a driver of price unrelated to location
falls outside it and is the residual the sensitivity analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity-12} carries.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Basis richness]\label{as:id-basis}
$h$ lies in the closed linear span of $L$, so that $\mathbb{E}[U\mid L,x]=U$ almost
surely. Concretely $L$ includes the discrete geographic identifiers (borough,
neighborhood, school, and street indicators) through which the encoder leaks
location; a coordinate-polynomial basis alone does not satisfy this when $h$ varies
discontinuously across geographic units.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Residual overlap]\label{as:id-overlap}
$\mathbb{E}\big[(T-\mathbb{E}[T\mid L,x])^2\big]>0$. The treatment retains variation
orthogonal to location; this is the identifying condition the erasure diagnostic
tests, and the one New York approaches as its text direction collapses onto
geography.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Exogeneity]\label{as:id-exog}
$\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon\mid T,\ell,x]=0$. No further driver of price is correlated with
the treatment given location and structure, the standard exogeneity condition of the
partially-linear model.
\end{assumption}

We take a deliberately limited view of what these four buy. Together
Assumptions~\ref{as:id-U} and~\ref{as:id-basis} make the confounding a function of
location that the control basis can span, which is what the designs of
Section~\ref{sec:results} hold fixed; what we do not assume is that the text carries no
confounder outside location, unmeasured interior quality above all, and against that
residual the effect is not identified but bounded, by the sensitivity analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity-12}. The identification is design-based against observed
location and sensitivity-bounded against the rest, one claim read from its two sides,
and the paper holds to that division throughout.

\begin{theorem}[Identification]\label{thm:id}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:id-U}--\ref{as:id-exog}, the treatment coefficient in
\eqref{eq:struct} equals the Robinson functional
\[
\theta=\frac{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\tilde Y\,\tilde T\right]}{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\tilde T^{2}\right]},
\qquad
\tilde Y=Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid L,x],\quad \tilde T=T-\mathbb{E}[T\mid L,x].
\]
The parameter $\theta$ is point-identified if and only if $\mathbb{E}[\tilde T^{2}]>0$;
the concept-erasure diagnostic probes whether the treatment retains the
location-orthogonal variation this requires, and as $R^2_{T\sim(L,x)}\to1$ the
denominator vanishes and identification fails.
\end{theorem}

The proof is a direct partialling-out computation. It absorbs the spatial
confounder into the control-measurable part of the model under
Assumptions~\ref{as:id-U} and \ref{as:id-basis}, and its necessity half shows that
a control basis missing the discrete geographic channel leaves the exact bias
$\beta bd/(d^2+1)$; the full argument is deferred to
Appendix~\ref{app:idproofs}. The location basis must therefore span the discrete
identifiers through which the encoder leaks geography, not coordinates alone, and
New York, whose leading text direction loads on borough and neighborhood names, is
the empirical instance of the boundary where $\mathbb{E}[\tilde T^2]$ approaches
zero and identification thins.
