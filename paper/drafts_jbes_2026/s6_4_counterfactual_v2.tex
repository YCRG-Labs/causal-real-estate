\subsection{Counterfactual rewriting total effect}
\label{sec:counterfactual-12}

The third identification strategy we deploy is a counterfactual
rewriting intervention. We treat the listing description as a
realized draw from a population of plausible descriptions for the
underlying property, and we identify the causal effect of language by
generating counterfactual descriptions in which the listing's
ZIP-code targeting is shifted to a held-out submarket while
preserving the property's structured attributes. The intervention is
executed at the language level by a frozen large language model that
takes the original description, structured attribute summary, and
target submarket as input, and returns a rewritten description that
preserves the structured-attribute information while shifting the
ZIP-code-targeted language. We then re-price the rewritten listing
under the same automated valuation model and report the difference
between the original and the rewritten predicted log price as the
counterfactual total effect.

\paragraph{Total effect across twelve markets.}
Table~\ref{tab:counterfactual-12} reports the counterfactual total
effect for each of the twelve metropolitan markets, together with
the bootstrap confidence interval and the binary indicator for zero
exclusion. The total effect excludes zero at the $95\%$ level in
eleven of the twelve markets, with only Atlanta returning a
confidence interval that straddles zero. The pooled per-listing
total effect across markets, weighted by inverse sampling variance,
is $-0.011$ on log price, with bootstrap confidence interval
$[-0.022, -0.001]$ and pooled sign-of-effect distribution that is
negative in seven markets and positive in five.

The largest negative total effect is in New York at $-0.081$ on log
price, corresponding to a median-price difference of $-\$76{,}814$
per swap, or $-7.8\%$ of the New York median. The next largest in
absolute magnitude is Seattle at $-0.048$ ($-\$37{,}275$, $-4.7\%$),
then DC at $-0.016$ ($-\$9{,}878$, $-1.6\%$). The largest positive
total effect is Philadelphia at $+0.012$ ($+\$3{,}958$, $+1.2\%$),
followed by Phoenix at $+0.006$ and Denver at $+0.005$.

\paragraph{Substantive interpretation.} The mixed-sign pattern across
the eleven markets in which the total effect excludes zero is
consistent with the two embedding-based channels' message that the
text-on-price relationship is heterogeneous across markets. The
counterfactual procedure reveals that the absolute magnitude of the
total effect is substantial in three markets (New York, Seattle, DC)
and small in the remaining eight, with the largest effect being
negative in New York and positive in Philadelphia. The negative
signs in eight markets indicate that swapping a listing's
ZIP-targeted language for a different submarket's language lowers
the predicted price; this is consistent with the listing-language
information channel being on average informative about
neighborhood-level price levels in a way that the structured
attributes alone do not capture.

\paragraph{Direct and indirect components.} A full mediation
decomposition of the counterfactual total effect into a direct
language-on-price channel and an indirect language-via-location
channel requires the per-listing predictions of both the original and
swapped models, which we report and analyze in
Appendix~\ref{app:cf-decomposition}. The mediation analysis
identifies that the natural direct effect, which holds the predicted
location fixed at its original value, is between one-quarter and
one-half of the total effect across markets, with the remaining
balance accounted for by the natural indirect effect through the
location-prediction channel. We refer the reader to the appendix for
the per-market decomposition table.
