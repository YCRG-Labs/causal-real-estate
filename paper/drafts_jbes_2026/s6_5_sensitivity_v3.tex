\subsection{Sensitivity to unobserved confounding}
\label{sec:sensitivity-12}

The Shen-Ross DML estimates in Section~\ref{sec:shen-12city} rest on
the conditional ignorability assumption that, after partialling out
the structured property covariates and the location indicators we
observe, residual variation in the listing language is exogenous to
residual variation in the log sale price. This assumption is not
directly testable. We follow \citet{cinelli2020making} and bound the
strength of any unobserved confounder that would be needed to
overturn the per-market findings, and we complement that bound with a
leave-one-out ablation of the spatial control block, which is the
observed covariate group most directly implicated in the
spatial-proxy concern that motivates this paper.

\paragraph{Robustness Value.} The Robustness Value (RV) of
\citeauthor{cinelli2020making} quantifies the minimum strength of an
unobserved confounder, measured as its partial $R^2$ on both the
outcome and the treatment, that would suffice to overturn the
estimate, so that higher RV means greater robustness to unobserved
confounding. Table~\ref{tab:confounder-sensitivity-12} reports the
per-market baseline RV alongside the long-DML point estimate against
which it is computed and the Cinelli-Hazlett $f^2$ benchmark of the
strongest observed covariate, for all twelve metropolitan markets in
the panel. Only San Francisco exhibits an RV above the $0.10$
threshold, at $0.165$, meaning an unobserved confounder would have to
explain more than sixteen percent of residual variance in both the
listing-uniqueness score and the log sale price to overturn the San
Francisco estimate. The remaining eleven markets fall below $0.10$,
led by Dallas at $0.094$, Chicago at $0.083$, and Phoenix at $0.066$,
and descending through Atlanta and Denver near $0.056$ and
Philadelphia at $0.053$ to New York at $0.025$, Boston at $0.023$,
and Seattle at $0.019$. The earlier description of five markets above
$0.10$ does not survive the clean rerun, and we report the single
market that clears the threshold rather than the inflated count.

The $f^2$ values, which place the strength of the most influential
\emph{observed} covariate on a comparable partial-$R^2$ scale, range
from about $0.0004$ in Seattle to $0.033$ in San Francisco. The
strongest covariate we actually measure therefore sits well below the
RV in every market, so an unobserved confounder would need to be
considerably stronger than any observed covariate to flip the
inference. This reassurance is most informative in San Francisco,
where both the RV and the room between it and the observed benchmark
are largest, and least informative in the low-RV markets, where
neither the observed nor the hypothetical-unobserved benchmark is
large enough to anchor a confident reading.

\paragraph{Spatial-block leave-one-out.} For each of the twelve
markets we drop the spatial control block, the latitude-longitude
coordinates together with the ZIP indicators, and recompute the DML
estimate, reporting the change in $\hat\theta$ relative to the
fully-controlled specification. The spatial controls move the
estimate most in Washington DC, where removing them shifts
$\hat\theta$ by $-0.173$, and next in Atlanta at $+0.060$, Chicago at
$+0.040$, and New York at $+0.033$. They move it least in Seattle at
$+0.003$, Boston at $-0.006$, Portland at $+0.006$, and San Francisco
at $+0.011$. The market with the highest RV is therefore also among
the least sensitive to its own location controls, which is the
pattern one would hope to see if the San Francisco estimate were
genuinely identified rather than carried by the spatial controls.

\paragraph{Fragility.} Under the Cinelli-Hazlett benchmarking
convention a city-block pair is flagged FRAGILE when the partial
$R^2$ of the dropped block on either the outcome or the treatment
exceeds the city's baseline RV, meaning a confounder no stronger than
the spatial controls we already include could in principle overturn
the estimate. Four of the twelve markets are flagged on the spatial
block: Boston, New York, Washington DC, and Atlanta. In each of the
four the flag is driven by the spatial block's explanatory power for
the outcome rather than for the treatment, with the block's partial
$R^2$ on log price reaching $0.153$ in Washington DC against a
baseline RV of $0.036$, and exceeding the RV by smaller margins in
New York, Atlanta, and Boston. San Francisco and the other markets
with the higher RV values carry no fragile flag, consistent with the
reading that confounding of strength comparable to the observed
spatial information would not displace those inferences.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The analysis is deliberately scoped to
the one observed covariate group the per-market panels share, the
spatial control block, which is also the block most directly tied to
the text-as-spatial-proxy mechanism this paper studies. The RV bounds
general unobserved confounding of the per-market estimate, while the
spatial leave-one-out localizes how much of each estimate rides on
measured location. With per-market samples ranging from about $990$
listings in San Francisco to roughly $15{,}000$ in New York, both
quantities are estimated with reasonable precision, so the
cross-market ordering is unlikely to be an artifact of sampling
noise. Read together, the two diagnostics point to the same
conclusion as Section~\ref{sec:shen-12city}: San Francisco is the
market in which the Shen-Ross effect is both the largest in absolute
terms and the most robust to unobserved confounding, the markets that
clear the spatial-fragility check are the ones where the point
estimate is already large relative to its location controls, and the
low-RV markets are those where modest confounding could overturn an
already small estimate. We report the per-market sensitivities as
informative about robustness rather than as a second identification
test, and we defer benchmarking of non-spatial confounder groups to
future work, since the covariates required to construct census,
crime, and amenity blocks are not uniformly available across the
twelve per-market panels.
