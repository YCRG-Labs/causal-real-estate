\subsection{Estimating on the identified direction}
\label{sec:identified-direction}

Theorem~\ref{thm:idprime} prescribes a direction. The effect it identifies is
carried by the component of the text that survives after the location-predictable
part of the embedding is removed, and the leading direction of largest variance,
which the Baur channel uses, need not coincide with it. We therefore re-estimate
the panel on the identified direction and read the difference as the portion of the
leading-component effect that geography accounts for.

The construction follows the theorem. Within each market we residualize every
coordinate of the sentence-BERT embedding on a location basis that includes a
quadratic in latitude and longitude together with a full set of zip indicators, the
discrete geographic channel Assumption~\ref{as:id-basis} requires the control basis
to span, and we take the leading principal component of the pooled residualized
embedding as the identified direction. It sits at a cosine of $0.96$ to the raw
leading component, close but not identical, which is consistent with the
decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:decomposition}: the two directions largely agree, so the
coefficient moves only modestly when geography enters the controls, but they do not
agree exactly, and the gap between them is exactly the confounded content the
theorem isolates.

Estimated on the identified direction, the effect survives at about two-thirds of
its leading-component magnitude. Pooling across markets by inverse variance, the
per-standard-deviation effect on log price is $+0.031$ ($t=17$) on the identified
direction against $+0.047$ ($t=22$) on the leading component, a ratio of $0.66$,
and the attenuation is similar across the markets where the effect is precisely
estimated, with a median ratio of $0.64$. The effect remains significant in nine of
the twelve markets and in every large one, Chicago at $+0.119$, Philadelphia at
$+0.065$, and New York at $+0.029$, each with $t$ above seven, while it thins to
marginal in the smallest markets, Portland and Seattle. The two markets whose
leading component was already close to the identified direction, San Francisco and
Washington, barely attenuate, and the two whose leading component was most
geographic, Chicago and Philadelphia, attenuate most, which is the pattern
Theorem~\ref{thm:idprime} predicts when a direction's confounded loading is what
drives the gap.

The reading this supports is the one the paper has argued throughout, now carried
out on the direction the theory identifies rather than the one variance selects.
The two-thirds that survives is text content orthogonal to location by
construction, so it cannot be laundered geography; the third that does not is the
confounded component the leading direction carries, and its size is now measured
rather than bounded. Two qualifications belong with the number. These estimates use
the reduced structural-and-coordinate control set rather than the full confounder
block behind the headline Baur table, so the levels are not the panel's reported
values and the transferable quantities are the roughly one-third geography share and
the survival of the effect. And residualizing on the full zip basis removes some
non-spatial variation along with the confound, so the identified estimate is a
conservative floor, with the deconfounded effect lying between it and the
leading-component estimate.
