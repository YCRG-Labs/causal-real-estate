\subsection{Estimating on the identified direction}
\label{sec:identified-direction}

Theorem~\ref{thm:idprime} prescribes a direction. The effect it identifies is
carried by the component of the text that survives after the location-predictable
part of the embedding is removed, and the leading direction of largest variance,
which the Baur channel uses, need not coincide with it. We re-estimate the panel on
the identified direction, holding the full confounder set fixed, and read the
difference from the leading-component estimate as the geography the leading
direction still carries.

The construction follows the theorem. Within each market we residualize every
coordinate of the sentence-BERT embedding on a location basis that includes a
quadratic in latitude and longitude together with a full set of zip indicators, the
discrete geographic channel Assumption~\ref{as:id-basis} requires the control basis
to span, and we take the leading principal component of the pooled residualized
embedding as the identified direction. It sits at a cosine of $0.98$ to the raw
leading component, so the two directions largely agree, which is why the
decomposition of Section~\ref{sec:decomposition} finds the coefficient moving only
modestly when geography enters the controls. Where they disagree is exactly the
confounded content the theorem isolates.

Estimated on the identified direction under the full confounder set, the effect is
robust. Market by market it stays close to its leading-component value, with a
median ratio near $0.9$, and it is significant in all twelve markets rather than
eleven. The direction earns its keep just where the leading component is most
geographic. Chicago and Philadelphia, whose leading directions carry the most
location, attenuate the most, from $+0.464$ to $+0.370$ and from $+0.374$ to
$+0.272$, while the markets whose leading component already lay near the identified
direction, San Francisco, Washington, and Denver among them, barely move. The most
telling case is New York, whose leading direction is the most geographic in the
panel and whose effect on that direction is a significant $-0.016$ ($t=-2.2$).
Removing the location channel does not merely restore the New York effect, it
reverses its sign: on the identified direction it is a significant $+0.021$
($t=3.3$). The variance-maximal component had been reporting a negative effect
because it pointed at borough and neighborhood names, whose price gradient runs the
other way, rather than at the text's own pricing content, and the identified
direction recovers the positive effect that geography had been masking with its own.

The reading this supports is the one the paper has argued throughout, now carried
out on the direction the theory identifies rather than the one variance selects.
The effect is not laundered geography, since it survives estimation on a direction
orthogonal to location by construction; and the theorem is not decorative, since it
changes the answer, trimming the inflated markets and recovering the suppressed one.
One qualification belongs with the numbers. Residualizing on the full zip basis
removes some non-spatial variation along with the confound, so for the markets that
attenuate the identified estimate is a conservative floor, with the deconfounded
effect lying between it and the leading-component value; the direction of the New
York correction, where the identified estimate exceeds the leading one, is not
subject to that caveat.

\paragraph{Spatially honest inference on the identified direction.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated-direction} holds that the estimated direction contributes
to the sampling error only at second order, provided it is formed out of sample and
the pooled corpus dominates each market in size, and that the interval to read is the
spatial-HAC one rather than the influence-function one. We realize both. Forming the
identified direction on the pooled embedding with the market in question left out, the
out-of-fold construction the theorem requires, reproduces the per-market estimates
above to within a hundredth of a log point in every market, the largest movement six
thousandths in Chicago, which is the second-order contribution of the direction made
visible: the point estimate barely moves when the direction is refit without the
market's own listings. The conditions the theorem asks of the pooled second moment
hold with room to spare, the leading eigenvalue of the residualized embedding
covariance running near twice the second and its effective rank near thirteen against
a pooled sample of sixty-nine thousand, so the spectral gap is clear and the effective
rank is negligible relative to the sample. The influence-function interval,
which treats within-market listings as independent, is then too narrow. Replacing it
with the Conley spatial-HAC standard error at a five-kilometre Bartlett cutoff, more
conservative than the zip clustering of Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city}, widens the
per-market intervals by a median factor near two, and the identified effect remains
significant in nine of the twelve markets, with $t$ statistics from $2.9$ in Portland
to $7.3$ in Philadelphia. The three that lose significance, Boston, New York, and
Seattle, are exactly the markets whose identified effect is closest to zero to begin
with. The pooled identified effect near eleven percent survives the widening with a
$t$ statistic above fourteen. The spatially honest inference the theorem prescribes
therefore leaves the reading intact: the effect the theory identifies is not an
accident of treating spatially clustered listings as independent.
