\subsection{Spatial deconfounding of BERT embeddings via LEACE and IGBP}
\label{sec:leace-12}

The sentence-BERT representation that underlies the Baur channel in
Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} encodes more than the language a listing agent
uses. Pre-trained on a generic corpus, the encoder has no particular incentive
to separate semantic content about a property's condition or amenities from the
geographic information that correlates with its neighborhood. If the embedding
carries location-targeted information into the leading principal component, the
per-standard-deviation effect reported in Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} inherits
a spatial confounder mediated through the encoder, and the identification claim
is correspondingly weaker. We assess how far this concern can be addressed by
erasing the lat-lon concept from the representation, and we report honestly where
the erasure is complete and where it is only partial.

We proceed in two stages. The first applies the LEACE projection of
\citet{belrose2023leace} to the BERT embedding, removing the linear subspace
identified by the mean-conditional-covariance estimator of the concept variable,
which here is the latitude-longitude coordinate pair of the listing. LEACE
attains exact linear guardedness, meaning that no linear predictor of the
residual embedding can recover the concept better than the constant baseline. We
verify this attainment in every market. The out-of-fold ridge $R^2$ for
predicting the coordinate pair from the embedding falls from a raw value as high
as $0.50$ in New York and $0.42$ in Dallas to essentially zero after erasure,
with every market's erased $R^2$ lying within $0.002$ of zero, and the
linear-guardedness residual sits at $2.7\text{e-}8$ on average across the twelve
markets, ranging from $8.3\text{e-}9$ to $6.0\text{e-}8$, at the floating-point
floor of the single-precision computation. Linear concept erasure is complete in
all twelve metropolitan markets.

The concept is routed through the continuous coordinate representation rather
than the categorical ZIP-code identifier in every market. Categorical LEACE has
known instability when the categorical cardinality is large, and our panel ranges
from $30$ ZIP codes in San Francisco to $158$ in New York. Although the
per-market test folds are now large, from $296$ listings in San Francisco to
$4{,}569$ in New York, the continuous routing sidesteps the cardinality question
entirely without discarding the spatial information the projection is designed to
remove, since the coordinate pair is a sufficient statistic for location at the
resolution that matters here.

The second stage applies the iterative gradient-based projection correction of
\citet{iskander2023shielded}, which targets a known limitation of LEACE.
Because LEACE removes only the linear subspace, the residual embedding can still
encode the concept through a nonlinear probe. IGBP alternates between fitting a
multilayer-perceptron probe of the coordinate pair on the residual and projecting
out the direction in which that probe accumulates predictive mass. The correction
reduces nonlinear leakage substantially but does not eliminate it. Averaged across
the twelve markets, the nonlinear probe $R^2$ falls from $0.44$ on the raw
embedding to $0.20$ after five outer iterations, a reduction of roughly one half.
The residual leakage varies markedly by market. In San Francisco the nonlinear
probe is left with an $R^2$ of $0.01$, essentially complete erasure, whereas in
New York, Dallas, and Phoenix the residual $R^2$ remains near $0.30$ to $0.34$,
and in Portland it is $0.26$. The markets retaining the most nonlinear leakage are
the larger corpora in which the listing language carries the richest
location-targeted structure, which is also where a nonlinear probe has the most
signal to recover.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The Baur PC1 estimates in
Table~\ref{tab:baur-12city} are reported on the unprojected embedding, consistent
with the published Baur, Rosenfelder, and Lutz protocol, so the deconfounding
analysis here is a diagnostic of the representation rather than a modification of
the per-market estimate. Read as a diagnostic, it delivers a clean conclusion at
the level that matters for the linear PC1 treatment and an honest caveat beyond
it. The leading principal component is a linear functional of the embedding, and
the linear erasure is exact in every market, so the spatial information that a
linear treatment could carry is removed completely. Residual nonlinear leakage
persists in several markets after IGBP, which we record as a scoped limitation:
it bounds the extent to which a nonlinear function of the deconfounded
representation could reintroduce location, and it is the reason we read the
per-market Baur pattern of Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} with a spatial-confounding
caveat rather than as a fully deconfounded causal estimate.
