\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated-direction}}
\label{app:estimated-direction-proof}

The argument composes four lemmas: a distributional rate for the estimated direction,
the resulting second-order negligibility of its error in the per-market estimate, the
asymptotic linearity of the fixed-direction estimator, and a central limit theorem for
the spatially dependent score. Throughout, $\tilde W_i$ is the residualized embedding
in market $k$, $\Sigma=\mathbb{E}[\tilde W\tilde W^\top]$ the pooled residualized
second moment with leading unit eigenvector $v^\ast$, $\hat v$ its sample analogue on
the pooled sample of size $N$, and $\theta_k(v)=v^\top a_k/(v^\top\Sigma_k v)$ the
direction-indexed estimand of \eqref{eq:thetav-estimable}.

\begin{lemma}[Direction rate and linear approximation]\label{lem:ed-direction}
Let $\hat v$ be the leading unit eigenvector of the pooled sample second moment of
sub-Gaussian residualized embeddings with a spectral gap $\bar g$ bounded away from
zero and effective rank $r(\Sigma)=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)/\lVert\Sigma\rVert$
satisfying $r(\Sigma)=o(\sqrt N)$. Then, orienting $\hat v$ toward $v^\ast$,
\[
\lVert\hat v-v^\ast\rVert=O_p\!\bigl(\sqrt{r(\Sigma)/N}\,\bigr),
\qquad
\sqrt N\,\langle\hat v-v^\ast,u\rangle\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N\!\bigl(0,\Gamma(u)\bigr)
\text{ for each fixed }u,
\]
where $\Gamma$ is the Koltchinskii--Lounici covariance kernel, and the projector bias
$\mathbb{E}\hat v-v^\ast$ is $O(r(\Sigma)/N)=o(N^{-1/2})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The residualized sentence-embedding is bounded, hence sub-Gaussian, so
\citet[Theorem~1]{koltchinskii2016asymptotics} gives $\lVert\hat\Sigma-\Sigma\rVert
\asymp\lVert\Sigma\rVert\max\{\sqrt{r(\Sigma)/N},\,r(\Sigma)/N\}$, and the
resolvent perturbation bound for a simple eigenvalue with gap $\bar g$ turns this into
$\lVert\hat v-v^\ast\rVert=O_p(\lVert\Sigma\rVert/(\bar g\sqrt N))=
O_p(\sqrt{r(\Sigma)/N})$ under the bounded relative gap. The linear-form central limit
theorem is their Theorem~7, whose conclusion is stated for the recentered eigenvector
$\sqrt{1+b}\,v^\ast$ with multiplicative bias $|b|\lesssim
(\lVert\Sigma\rVert/\bar g)^2 r(\Sigma)/N$. By their Remark~4 the recentering is
unnecessary once $r(\Sigma)=o(\sqrt N)$, since then $b=o(N^{-1/2})$ and the projector
bias $\lVert\mathbb{E}\hat v-v^\ast\rVert=O(r(\Sigma)/N)=o(N^{-1/2})$, so centering at
$v^\ast$ itself gives the stated normal limit at rate $N^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The direction error is second order]\label{lem:ed-second-order}
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-direction} and Assumption~\ref{as:ed-rate}, with $\hat v$
formed out of sample relative to market $k$,
\[
\sqrt{n_k}\,\bigl(\theta_k(\hat v)-\theta_k(v^\ast)\bigr)=o_p(1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map $v\mapsto\theta_k(v)=v^\top a_k/(v^\top\Sigma_k v)$ is twice continuously
differentiable at $v^\ast$ with $v^{\ast\top}\Sigma_k v^\ast$ bounded away from zero by
positivity, and a second-order Taylor expansion gives $\theta_k(\hat
v)=\theta_k(v^\ast)+(\partial\theta_k/\partial v)^\top(\hat v-v^\ast)+O_p(\lVert\hat
v-v^\ast\rVert^2)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-direction} the linear term is
$O_p(\sqrt{r(\Sigma)/N})$ and the remainder is $O_p(r(\Sigma)/N)$, so
$\sqrt{n_k}(\theta_k(\hat v)-\theta_k(v^\ast))=O_p(\sqrt{n_k\,r(\Sigma)/N})=o_p(1)$
under Assumption~\ref{as:ed-rate}, $n_k\,r(\Sigma)/N\to0$. Because $\hat v$ is fit on a
sample independent of the observations forming $\theta_k(\cdot)$ in market $k$, the
linear term is uncorrelated with the market-$k$ score, so it contributes neither a
first-order term nor a covariance to the limiting variance. This is the point at which
a generated regressor formed in sample would instead leave the correction of
\citet{pagan1984econometric} and \citet{hahn2013asymptotic}; the out-of-fold, pooled
construction removes it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-direction asymptotic linearity]\label{lem:ed-linearity}
Fix the direction at $v^\ast$. Under Assumption~\ref{as:ed-spatial} the cross-fitted
Robinson estimator $\hat\theta_k(v^\ast)$ is asymptotically linear,
\[
\sqrt{n_k}\,\bigl(\hat\theta_k(v^\ast)-\theta_k(v^\ast)\bigr)
=n_k^{-1/2}\sum_{i\in k}\psi_i+o_p(1),
\qquad
\psi_i=\mathbb{E}[\tilde T^2]^{-1}\,\tilde T_i\,(\tilde Y_i-\theta_k\tilde T_i),
\]
with the nuisance estimation error entering at second order.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $v^\ast$ fixed the treatment $T=\tilde W^\top v^\ast$ is a fixed scalar summary and
the estimator is the partially-linear Robinson estimator of
\citet[Theorems~3.1--3.2]{chernozhukov2018double}. The partialling-out moment is
Neyman-orthogonal in the outcome and treatment regressions, so under the cross-fitting
and product-rate conditions of Assumption~\ref{as:ed-spatial} the first-stage error
enters only at second order and the estimator admits the stated influence-function
representation. This is the same argument that yields
Theorem~\ref{thm:decomp}\,(i) for the decomposition contrast.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spatial central limit theorem for the score]\label{lem:ed-spatial-clt}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:ed-spatial}, writing $\sigma_{n_k}^2=\operatorname{Var}
(\sum_{i\in k}\psi_i)$,
\[
\sigma_{n_k}^{-1}\sum_{i\in k}\psi_i\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
and $\sigma_{n_k}^2/n_k$ is consistently estimated by a spatial
heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent estimator with a distance kernel.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Index listings by their coordinates in $\mathbb{R}^2$. The minimum-distance clause of
Assumption~\ref{as:ed-spatial} is the increasing-domain condition of
\citet[Assumption~1]{jenish2009central}, ruling out infill. Each score $\psi_i$ is a
measurable function of the covariates and shocks of a bounded geographic neighborhood
of listing $i$, and $\alpha$-mixing is preserved under measurable transformation, so
$\{\psi_i\}$ inherits the $\alpha$-mixing structure of the underlying field with the
summable coefficients Assumption~\ref{as:ed-spatial} imposes. With the uniform
$L^{2+\delta}$ integrability of $\psi_i$ and the non-degeneracy $\liminf n_k^{-1}
\sigma_{n_k}^2>0$, \citet[Theorem~1]{jenish2009central} gives the stated central limit
theorem. Consistency of the distance-kernel variance estimator is standard for such
mixing fields \citep{conley1999gmm,bester2011inference}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:estimated-direction}]
Because $\hat v$ is formed out of fold, condition on it; on the market-$k$ sample it is
then a fixed direction, and
\[
\sqrt{n_k}\bigl(\hat\theta_k(\hat v)-\theta_k(v^\ast)\bigr)
=\sqrt{n_k}\bigl(\hat\theta_k(\hat v)-\theta_k(\hat v)\bigr)
+\sqrt{n_k}\bigl(\theta_k(\hat v)-\theta_k(v^\ast)\bigr).
\]
The second term is the estimand difference that Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-second-order} bounds,
hence $o_p(1)$. The first term is the sampling error of the fixed-direction Robinson
estimator at the conditionally fixed $\hat v$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-linearity} it is
asymptotically linear with influence function $\psi_i$ evaluated at $\hat v$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-spatial-clt} its normalized sum is asymptotically normal with variance
$V_k=\lim n_k^{-1}\sigma_{n_k}^2$. Since $\hat v\xrightarrow{p}v^\ast$ and $\psi_i$ is
continuous in the direction, that variance is the fixed-direction value at $v^\ast$.
Combining, $\sqrt{n_k}(\hat\theta_k(\hat v)-\theta_k(v^\ast))\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal
N(0,V_k)$, estimated by the spatial-HAC estimator of Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-spatial-clt}.
\end{proof}

The conditions the proof imposes are met in the panel. The pooled residualized
embedding covariance has a leading eigenvalue near twice the second, so the spectral
gap $\bar g$ is bounded away from zero, and its effective rank is $r(\Sigma)\approx
13.2$ against $N=69{,}204$, so $r(\Sigma)/\sqrt N\approx0.05$ and the condition
$r(\Sigma)=o(\sqrt N)$ that
Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-direction} needs for clean centering holds with the projector bias
$b\approx8\times10^{-4}$. The stronger rate that Lemma~\ref{lem:ed-second-order} invokes, $n_k\,r(\Sigma)/N\to0$,
is more demanding than that centering condition, and in the finite panel the largest
market stresses it, since New York's $n_k=15{,}270$ gives $n_k\,r(\Sigma)/N\approx2.9$
rather than a small number. That the direction error is nonetheless second order there
we read from the data rather than the worst-case rate: refitting the direction with each
market left out moves its estimate by at most six thousandths of a log point, in
Chicago, and by less than one thousandth at the median, well inside the spatial-HAC
intervals that Section~\ref{sec:identified-direction} reports.
