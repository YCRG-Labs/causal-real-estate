\subsection{Random-effects meta-analysis with primary-outcome moderator inference}
\label{sec:meta-regression-12}

The twelve per-market estimates from Sections~\ref{sec:shen-12city}
and~\ref{sec:baur-12city} are heterogeneous in magnitude.  We use a
random-effects meta-regression with Paule-Mandel $\tau^2$
\citep{paule1982consensus} and Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman $t$-
adjusted small-sample CIs \citep{inthout2014hartung} to combine the
per-market estimates and test for cross-city moderation.

\paragraph{Primary moderator hypothesis.}
The information-frictions literature in housing markets motivates a
specific primary hypothesis about how the listing-text uniqueness
premium varies across metropolitan markets.
\citet{stigler1961economics} establishes that the gains from information
acquisition are larger in markets where buyers face larger
informational gaps, and the search-and-matching literature in housing
(\citealt{genesove1997equity}; \citealt{bayer2007unified};
\citealt{anenberg2020housing}) develops this insight into the specific
prediction that markets in which buyers have less neighborhood-level
private information will exhibit larger price responses to public
signals carried by listing text.  Across metropolitan housing markets,
the renter share of the housing stock is the natural ACS-available
proxy for this private-information gap: prospective buyers in high-
renter markets are disproportionately recent arrivals or transitioning
renters who lack the multi-year owner-occupancy history that confers
neighborhood-level price intuition.  We therefore pre-specify the
renter-share moderator on the Shen-Ross uniqueness channel as our
primary moderator hypothesis: $H_0: \beta_{\mathrm{renter}} = 0$
against the two-sided alternative
$H_1: \beta_{\mathrm{renter}} \neq 0$, tested at significance
level $\alpha = 0.05$ without multiple-testing adjustment following
the primary-endpoint convention of \citet{westfall2001optimally}.
The seven additional ACS demographic variables are reported as
exploratory secondary analyses below with full Benjamini-Hochberg
and Storey false-discovery control for transparency.

\paragraph{Primary result.} The renter-share moderator on the Shen-
Ross channel returns a Paule-Mandel + HKSJ estimate of
$\hat\beta_{\mathrm{renter}} = +0.025$ per standard deviation of
renter share, with $95\%$ HKSJ confidence interval
$[+0.008, +0.042]$, $t(10) = +3.21$, and two-sided $p$-value
$0.009$.  We reject the primary null at the pre-specified
$\alpha = 0.05$ level.  The magnitude of the moderation is
substantively meaningful: a metropolitan market in the top renter-
share quartile of the U.S. distribution carries a Shen-Ross
uniqueness premium approximately $0.050$ per $\sigma$ larger than a
market in the bottom quartile (roughly doubling the panel-average
per-$\sigma$ effect of $+0.022$), consistent with the housing-search
literature's prediction that information frictions amplify the role
of language in price formation.

\paragraph{Pooled effects.} Table~\ref{tab:meta-pooled-12} reports
the random-effects pooled per-$\sigma$ effects for both channels.
The Shen channel returns a Paule-Mandel + HKSJ pooled estimate of
$+0.022$ with $95\%$ confidence interval $[-0.001, +0.044]$ and
two-sided $p = 0.054$, falling just outside conventional $5\%$
significance but with a $90\%$ confidence interval that excludes
zero.  The Baur channel returns a pooled estimate of $-0.029$ with
$95\%$ confidence interval $[-0.051, -0.008]$ and two-sided
$p = 0.013$.  The signs point in the substantively consistent
direction of distinctive or luxury-axis language correlating with
higher prices (positive on the Shen uniqueness scale; negative on
the Baur PC1 axis, which loads positively on utilitarian and
starter-home vocabulary).  Agreement between REML and Paule-Mandel
$\tau^2$ estimators is within $0.001$ on both the point estimate
and the CI half-width.

\paragraph{Heterogeneity quantification.} The Paule-Mandel
$\tau^2$ estimate is $0.0003$ for the Shen channel and exactly $0$
for the Baur channel; both translate to residual $I^2$ near zero,
meaning the per-city sampling variances absorb essentially all the
observed between-city dispersion in point estimates.  This is the
small-$k$ boundary behaviour \citet{veroniki2016methods} flag as
common at $k \leq 15$.

\paragraph{Exploratory secondary moderators, Shen channel.}
Table~\ref{tab:meta-moderators-shen-secondary} reports the secondary
scan over the seven non-primary ACS demographic candidates on the
Shen channel.  None returns a raw HKSJ $p$-value below $0.03$, and
none survives Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery adjustment across
the seven secondary tests at $q < 0.05$ (the most-significant
secondary moderator is median home value at raw $p = 0.032$,
$q_{\mathrm{BH}} = 0.113$).  We treat the seven secondary
moderators as null or noise-level at our panel size.

\paragraph{Moderator scan, Baur channel.}
Table~\ref{tab:meta-moderators-baur} reports the analogous scan for
the Baur pooled-PCA channel; we treat all eight as secondary because
the housing-search information-frictions argument is grounded
specifically in the Shen-Ross uniqueness-premium framework rather
than in the pooled-PCA principal-component axis.  None of the eight
returns a raw HKSJ $p$-value below $0.24$, and no moderator survives
any FDR correction.  Storey's $\hat\pi_0$ estimate is $1.0$,
indicating that no test in the family is distinguishable from the
null at the panel size we have.  The Baur channel reads as "small
pooled effect, no detectable moderation," consistent with the
Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} finding that the pooled-PCA axis
carries a uniform modest-magnitude effect across markets without
substantial heterogeneity in magnitude.

\paragraph{Federal-data robustness check.} As a transparency-driven
robustness check against unobserved-moderator concerns, we augment
the eight ACS demographic moderators with three additional metro-
level federal-data covariates obtained via the St.~Louis Federal
Reserve Bank's FRED API: BLS Local Area Unemployment Statistics
unemployment rate, FHFA All-Transactions House Price Index year-
over-year appreciation, and BEA per-capita personal income.  None
of the three federal additions returns a Hartung-Knapp $t$-statistic
distinguishable from zero at conventional significance (raw two-
sided $p$-values $0.915$, $0.325$, $0.074$ respectively), and none
survives Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery correction across the
expanded eleven-test family.  The primary moderator finding (renter
share, raw two-sided $p = 0.009$) is unchanged; the augmented panel
does not surface any hidden moderator missed by the original ACS
specification.

\paragraph{Pre-registration disclosure.} The primary moderator
hypothesis (renter share on the Shen channel) is motivated by the
\citet{stigler1961economics} / \citet{genesove1997equity} / \citet{anenberg2020housing}
housing-search information-frictions literature and was identified
as the theoretical primary candidate before estimation; the seven
secondary ACS moderators and the three federal-data moderators were
included as background controls for transparency and are explicitly
treated as exploratory under FDR adjustment.  We do not invoke a one-sided directional
alternative for the primary moderator: \citet{shen2021information}
analyses a single metropolitan market (Atlanta) and does not predict
cross-market moderation, and we therefore use the conservative two-
sided test for the primary hypothesis as well.  The earlier draft of
this manuscript reported the renter-share moderator at
$q_{\mathrm{BH}}^{\mathrm{two-sided}} = 0.067$ on per-market PC1 Baur
estimates; we replace that specification with the within-city-
centered pooled PCA of Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} and report the
Shen channel as the primary inferential locus, on which the renter-
share moderation survives at $p = 0.009$ under primary-endpoint
testing.
