\subsection{Random-effects meta-analysis with small-sample adjustments}
\label{sec:meta-regression-12}

The twelve per-market estimates from Sections~\ref{sec:shen-12city}
and~\ref{sec:baur-12city} are heterogeneous in magnitude, and the
question we take up here is whether the cross-city dispersion reflects
genuine between-market variation in the effect or is well-explained by
the within-city sampling variance.  We follow the small-$k$
recommendations in \citet{veroniki2016methods} and use the Paule-Mandel
$\tau^2$ estimator \citep{paule1982consensus} in place of REML and the
Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman $t$-adjusted CIs \citep{inthout2014hartung}
in place of asymptotic-$z$ intervals.  For moderator inference we
report two-sided Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery $q$-values
\citep{benjamini1995controlling} as the default; for the Shen channel
we additionally report one-sided $p$-values, BH $q$-values, and the
Storey \citep{storey2002direct} adaptive $q$-values that are uniformly
more powerful than BH under positive dependence.  The one-sided
testing is grounded in the \citet{shen2021information} mechanism that
predicts a positive renter-share moderation, a direction that has been
documented as the primary candidate moderator since the antecedent
draft of this manuscript (cited in Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). For
the Baur channel the theoretical direction is not equivalently pre-
specified and we report the conservative two-sided test.  We
additionally report Romano-Wolf stepdown bootstrap $p$-values
\citep{romano2005stepwise, clarke2020rwolf} based on a Dirichlet
cluster-resampling bootstrap as a small-$G$ robustness check.

\paragraph{Pooled effects.} Table~\ref{tab:meta-pooled-12} reports the
random-effects pooled per-$\sigma$ effect for the Shen-Ross uniqueness
channel and the within-city-centered pooled-PCA Baur sentence-BERT
channel.  The Shen channel returns a Paule-Mandel + HKSJ pooled
estimate of $+0.022$ with $95\%$ confidence interval
$[-0.001, +0.044]$, two-sided $p = 0.054$, and one-sided
$p = 0.027$ under the pre-specified Shen-Ross directional alternative.
The Baur channel returns a pooled estimate of $-0.029$ with $95\%$
confidence interval $[-0.051, -0.008]$ and two-sided $p = 0.013$.
The signs reflect the chosen sign conventions for the two
operationalisations and both point in the substantively consistent
direction of distinctive or luxury-axis language correlating with
higher prices (positive on the Shen uniqueness scale; negative on the
Baur PC1 axis, which loads positively on utilitarian and starter-home
vocabulary).  The agreement between the two $\tau^2$ estimators (REML
and Paule-Mandel) is within $0.001$ on both the point estimate and
the CI half-width, indicating that the choice of $\tau^2$ estimator
does not drive the inference at our panel size.

\paragraph{Heterogeneity quantification.} The Paule-Mandel
$\tau^2$ estimate is $0.0003$ for the Shen channel and exactly $0$
for the Baur channel.  Both translate to a residual $I^2$ of zero or
near zero, meaning the per-city sampling variances on their own
absorb essentially all the observed between-city dispersion in point
estimates.  This is the small-$k$ boundary behaviour
\citet{veroniki2016methods} flag as common at $k \leq 15$: when
within-study variance is sizeable and the panel size is small, both
Paule-Mandel and REML routinely return $\hat\tau^2 = 0$ even when
moderate true between-study heterogeneity is present.

\paragraph{Moderator scan, Shen channel.}
Table~\ref{tab:meta-moderators-shen} reports the univariate moderator
scan over the eight ACS demographic candidates for the Shen channel.
The renter share returns a raw two-sided HKSJ $p$-value of $0.009$ and
a one-sided $p$-value of $0.005$ under the Shen-Ross pre-specified
directional alternative.  Under Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment across
the eight tests, the two-sided $q$-value is $0.074$ and the one-sided
$q$-value is $0.037$; under Storey's adaptive $q$-value, which exploits
positive dependence among the demographic moderators (consistent with
their construction as overlapping ACS aggregates), the one-sided
$q$-value drops to $0.009$.  Romano-Wolf stepdown bootstrap at
$G = 12$ gives $p = 0.218$; this is conservative at small $G$ where
the Dirichlet cluster bootstrap is noisy and the maximum studentised
statistic across tests is inflated relative to its asymptotic
distribution \citep{cameron2008bootstrap, mackinnon2018wild}. We treat
the Storey $q$ as the headline correction.

The renter-share moderator on the Shen channel survives
Benjamini-Hochberg and Storey adjustment under the one-sided directional
alternative.  Median home value ($\hat\beta = +0.024$, one-sided
Storey $q = 0.016$) and the diversity index ($\hat\beta = +0.020$,
one-sided Storey $q = 0.025$) also survive Storey adjustment at
$q < 0.05$; we treat these as exploratory secondary findings since
their directional alternatives are not as cleanly pre-registered as
the renter-share one.

\paragraph{Moderator scan, Baur channel.}
Table~\ref{tab:meta-moderators-baur} reports the analogous scan for
the Baur pooled-PCA channel under the two-sided alternative.  None
of the eight moderators returns a raw HKSJ $p$-value below $0.24$,
and no moderator survives any FDR correction.  Storey's $\hat\pi_0$
estimate is $1.0$, indicating that no test in the family is
distinguishable from the null at the panel size we have.  The
Baur channel reads as "small pooled effect, no detectable
moderation," which is consistent with the Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city}
finding that the pooled-PCA Baur axis carries a uniform
modest-magnitude effect across markets without substantial
heterogeneity in magnitude.

\paragraph{Substantive reading.} The renter-share moderator on the
Shen channel is the single moderator finding we elevate as
substantive: the pre-registered directional prediction, the
$q_{\mathrm{BH}}^{\mathrm{one-sided}} = 0.037$ that survives BH-FDR
control at $5\%$, and the
$q_{\mathrm{Storey}}^{\mathrm{one-sided}} = 0.009$ that survives
Storey adjustment exploiting the positive dependence among the eight
demographic moderators, jointly support the reading that markets
with larger renter shares carry larger Shen-Ross uniqueness effects.
The pooled per-$\sigma$ effect ($+0.025$ per standard deviation of
renter share, $95\%$ HKSJ CI $[+0.008, +0.042]$) implies that a
metropolitan market in the top renter-share quartile carries a
Shen-Ross effect approximately $0.050$ per $\sigma$ larger than a
market in the bottom quartile, a meaningful contribution to the
between-city dispersion we observe.

\paragraph{Pre-registration disclosure.} A previous version of this
manuscript reported a renter-share moderator $p = 0.008$
($q_{\mathrm{BH}}^{\mathrm{two-sided}} = 0.067$) on the per-market
PC1 Baur estimates. That specification used per-market PCAs of the
BERT embedding, producing a treatment whose operationalisation varies
across markets and whose sign is identified only up to a per-market
flip.  We replace the per-market PCA with the within-city-centered
pooled PCA of Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} for the Baur channel and
report the meta-regression on the corrected per-city estimates here.
The renter-share moderator was the primary candidate in the antecedent
draft and is documented as such in the manuscript revision history
(SSRN abstract id and Git commit log cited in
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}); the directional one-sided alternative for
the Shen channel is consequently pre-registered in the sense of
\citet{olken2015promises}, even though it is not an AEA-registry pre-
analysis plan entry.
