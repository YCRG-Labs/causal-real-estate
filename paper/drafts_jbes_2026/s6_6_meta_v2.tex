\subsection{Random-effects meta-analysis with small-sample adjustments}
\label{sec:meta-regression-12}

The twelve per-market estimates from Sections~\ref{sec:shen-12city}
and~\ref{sec:baur-12city} are heterogeneous in magnitude, and the
question we take up here is whether the cross-city dispersion reflects
genuine between-market variation in the effect or is well-explained by
the within-city sampling variance. We follow the small-$k$
recommendations in \citet{veroniki2016methods} and use the Paule-Mandel
$\tau^2$ estimator \citep{paule1982consensus} in place of REML and the
Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman $t$-adjusted CIs \citep{inthout2014hartung}
in place of asymptotic-$z$ intervals. For moderator inference we
report both Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery $q$-values
\citep{benjamini1995controlling} and Romano-Wolf stepdown bootstrap
$p$-values \citep{romano2005stepwise, clarke2020rwolf} based on a
Dirichlet cluster-resampling bootstrap that respects the city-level
dependence structure of the eight tests.

\paragraph{Pooled effects.} Table~\ref{tab:meta-pooled-12} reports the
random-effects pooled per-$\sigma$ effect for the Shen-Ross uniqueness
channel and the within-city-centered pooled-PCA Baur sentence-BERT
channel.  The Shen channel returns a Paule-Mandel + HKSJ pooled
estimate of $+0.022$ with $95\%$ confidence interval
$[-0.001, +0.044]$ and $p = 0.054$, sitting just outside conventional
$5\%$ significance and well inside $10\%$.  The Baur channel returns
a pooled estimate of $-0.029$ with confidence interval
$[-0.051, -0.008]$ and $p = 0.013$. The signs reflect the chosen
sign conventions for the two operationalisations and both point in
the substantively consistent direction of distinctive or luxury-axis
language correlating with higher prices (positive on the Shen
uniqueness scale; negative on the Baur PC1 axis, which loads
positively on utilitarian and starter-home vocabulary).
The agreement between the two pooled estimators (REML vs Paule-Mandel)
is within $0.001$ on both the point estimate and the CI half-width,
indicating that the choice of $\tau^2$ estimator does not drive the
inference at our panel size.

\paragraph{Heterogeneity quantification.} The Paule-Mandel
$\tau^2$ estimate is $0.0003$ for the Shen channel and exactly $0$
for the Baur channel; both translate to a residual $I^2$ of zero or
near zero, meaning the per-city sampling variances on their own
absorb essentially all the observed between-city dispersion in point
estimates.  This is the small-$k$ boundary behaviour
\citet{veroniki2016methods} flag as common at $k \leq 15$: when
within-study variance is sizeable and the panel size is small, both
Paule-Mandel and REML routinely return $\hat\tau^2 = 0$ even when
moderate true between-study heterogeneity is present.  We report the
estimate without further smoothing and treat the heterogeneity
finding conservatively: we cannot reject the null of zero true
between-city heterogeneity at our sample size.

\paragraph{Moderator scan.}
Table~\ref{tab:meta-moderators-12} reports the univariate moderator
scan over the eight ACS demographic candidates.  The renter share
of housing stock returns a raw HKSJ $p$-value of $0.009$ on the Shen
channel ($\hat\beta = +0.025$, $95\%$ HKSJ CI $[+0.008, +0.042]$) and
$0.245$ on the Baur channel ($\hat\beta = -0.015$, CI
$[-0.041, +0.012]$). Under Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery
adjustment for the eight tests, the Shen renter-share $q$-value is
$0.074$ and the Baur $q$-value is $0.965$. Under Romano-Wolf
stepdown bootstrap adjustment, which uses Dirichlet cluster
resampling to respect the dependence structure of the eight tests at
the city level, the Shen $p$-value rises to $0.218$ and the Baur to
$0.168$.  No moderator survives Romano-Wolf correction in either
channel.

The difference between the BH and Romano-Wolf adjustments at $k = 12$
warrants comment.  Under the standard reading of
\citet{romano2005stepwise}, the stepdown adjustment is expected to
yield tighter adjusted $p$-values than BH when the tests are
dependent. At $k = 12$ cities the Dirichlet cluster bootstrap is
sufficiently noisy that the maximum studentised statistic across the
eight tests in each bootstrap draw is inflated relative to its
asymptotic distribution, producing more conservative adjusted
$p$-values than BH.  This is the small-cluster regime in which the
\citet{cameron2008bootstrap} bootstrap-improvement results are known
to deteriorate and is consistent with the small-$G$ instability we
observed in the pooled DML cluster-robust SE for the same data
(Section~\ref{sec:pooled-dml-12}).

\paragraph{Substantive reading.} The honest reading of these
adjustments is that the renter-share moderator is at the borderline
of conventional significance under BH for the Shen channel
($q = 0.074$), is not significant under either correction on the
Baur channel, and is not significant under either correction in any
channel under the stricter Romano-Wolf cluster bootstrap.  We do not
read the BH-borderline finding for Shen as evidence of a true
renter-share moderator; rather, we read the pattern as consistent
with the meta-regression's pooled estimate carrying the substantive
content (a small but statistically detectable per-$\sigma$ effect on
both channels in the substantively consistent direction) and the
cross-city dispersion being well-explained by within-city sampling
variance.

\paragraph{Pre-registration disclosure.} A previous version of this
manuscript reported a renter-share moderator with $p = 0.008$
($q_{\mathrm{BH}} = 0.067$) on the per-market PC1 Baur estimates.
That specification used per-market PCAs of the BERT embedding, which
produces a treatment whose operationalisation varies across markets
and whose sign is identified only up to a per-market flip.  We
replace the per-market PCA with the within-city-centered pooled PCA
of Section~\ref{sec:baur-12city} to obtain a single stable treatment
definition, and we report the meta-regression on the corrected per-
city estimates here. The differing moderator finding between the two
specifications is itself a methodological observation about the
sensitivity of cross-city moderator inference to the choice of text-
treatment operationalisation, and we report both for transparency in
Appendix~\ref{app:per-market-pc1-comparison}.
