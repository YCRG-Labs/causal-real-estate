\begin{abstract}

Automated valuation models increasingly rely on natural-language descriptions of
properties, and text embeddings added to geographic controls deliver sizable
gains in predictive fit. Whether those gains reflect causal semantic content or
spatial confounding mediated through language, and whether the answer travels
across metropolitan markets, has remained an open question. We develop a
framework that separates the two and apply it across twelve metropolitan areas
spanning the major United States housing-market typologies, with no market
excluded, using $3{,}851$ deduplicated Redfin listings matched to geocoded parcel
records whose confounders we winsorize at the median plus or minus five median
absolute deviations to guard against parcel data-entry artifacts. The framework
combines a partially-linear Double Machine Learning estimator with cross-fitted
gradient-boosted nuisances, LEACE linear concept erasure with an iterative
gradient polish for residual nonlinear leakage, a counterfactual rewriting
intervention executed with two open-weight large language models, and a
finite-sample calibration study that ports the \citet{ballinari2024improving}
Design 4 stress panel alongside an application-specific coverage panel. Listing
text carries a detectable and positive per-standard-deviation association with
log sale price that is robust to the choice of text encoding. A Doc2Vec
uniqueness channel pools to $+0.072$ (random-effects $95\%$ CI $[+0.017, +0.128]$)
and a within-city-centered sentence-BERT principal-component axis to $+0.156$
($[+0.063, +0.249]$), and across markets the two channels are nearly collinear,
with a Pearson correlation of $+0.94$, so their agreement is evidence that the
signal does not depend on the encoding rather than evidence from two independent
sources. The signal is highly heterogeneous, from near zero in New York to
several times the pooled mean in Philadelphia and Chicago, and a Hotelling
joint-null test that corrects for the cross-channel collinearity rejects the
no-effect null in all twelve markets. A counterfactual that strips stylistic
content while holding the model's inferred location fixed isolates a positive
contrast of $+0.074$ on log price, while a contrast that also swaps the targeted
submarket nets to approximately zero, so listing language moves the deployed
valuation model along two routes of comparable size and opposite sign. Linear
spatial information is fully erasable from the embedding in every market, with
residual nonlinear leakage that the iterative polish halves but does not
eliminate in the largest corpora, and no metropolitan demographic moderator
survives multiplicity and leverage diagnostics at twelve markets. We read the
evidence as associational rather than cleanly causal, bounded by a sensitivity
analysis under which a modest unmeasured confounder would overturn the per-market
estimates, and we conclude that predictive-only evaluation of NLP-augmented
valuation cannot separate semantic content from the spatial information that
linear concept erasure removes.

\end{abstract}
