\begin{abstract}

Automated valuation models increasingly read the prose of a property listing, and
the text reliably improves price prediction, yet predictive fit alone cannot
reveal whether the gain reflects genuine semantic content or the neighborhood
geography that language quietly encodes. We treat the listing embedding as a
high-dimensional treatment and develop a framework that asks how much of its
association with sale price survives once geographic location is erased from the
representation, joining a Robinson partially-linear Double Machine Learning
estimand with cross-fitted gradient-boosted nuisances, linear concept erasure of
the latitude-longitude coordinate in closed form, and an iterative nonlinear
erasure that exposes the residual leakage a linear projector leaves behind. Two findings
anchor the framework. The first is substantive. Although predictive fit cannot
tell genuine semantic content apart from the spatial information that listing
language also encodes, a direct deconfounding test can, and the answer is
reassuring rather than damning: the embedding does linearly encode location,
recoverable at an $R^2$ as high as one half, yet holding the text treatment fixed
and adjusting for that location leaves its per-standard-deviation price effect
almost unchanged in eleven of twelve markets, so the text-price signal is mostly
genuine semantic content rather than relabeled geography. The second is
inferential: the influence-function standard error that Double Machine Learning
reports for an embedding-valued, generated-regressor treatment undercovers at the
sample sizes typical of a single metropolitan market, an effect we document in a
calibration study and for which we recommend a bootstrap that resamples the entire
pipeline. Applying the framework
across twelve metropolitan areas with $3{,}851$ deduplicated Redfin listings, we
find that text carries a positive per-standard-deviation association with log
price that is robust to the choice of encoder and to spatial deconfounding,
strongly heterogeneous across markets, and unaccompanied by any demographic
moderator that survives multiplicity and leverage diagnostics. We are explicit
about what this does and does not establish, because the estimates are
associational rather than cleanly causal and a sensitivity analysis quantifies how
weak an unmeasured confounder would need to be to overturn each market's estimate.
The contribution is a disciplined measurement-and-deconfounding protocol with
calibrated inference for an embedding treatment under spatial confounding, not a
causal price elasticity.

\end{abstract}
