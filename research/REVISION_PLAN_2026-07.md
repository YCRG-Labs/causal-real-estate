# Revision plan, July 2026

Findings from an audit of the paper against the artifacts in `results/`, plus the
fixes already applied. Ordered by severity. Everything below was checked against
committed files or reproduced by running the repo's own code; nothing is inferred
from the manuscript alone.

## Blocking: nine of twelve markets do not reproduce

`load_analysis_data` looks for `data/processed/{city}_parcels_micro_geo.gpkg` or
`{city}_parcels_amenities.gpkg`. Those files exist only for **boston, nyc, sf**.
For the other nine markets `parcels` is `None`, `_spatial_join_parcels` never
runs, and `get_features_and_target` falls back to a confounder matrix of
**latitude and longitude only** (`conf.shape[1] == 2`, `has_rich_confounders`
False).

Consequence: running `baur_pooled_pca.py --city philadelphia --fast` today gives
`|θ| = 0.6057`, against the committed `0.4108`. Of the three markets that still
have parcels, Boston and SF reproduce to within drift (`0.0733` vs `0.0722`, at
identical n=2610; `0.1056` vs `0.1009`, at identical n=986). NYC does not: n moves
from 15227 to 15255 and `|θ|` from `0.0188` to `0.0088`, so the published NYC
interval excludes zero and the rerun's does not. That n shift is the June 13
embedding regeneration (`b96f985`), which rebuilt the per-market parquets after
the results were written at 17:13 that day.

The nine-market results were evidently computed when those parcel files existed.
They are not in the tree, not in git, and not in `release/`. Until they are
restored the paper's central table cannot be regenerated by anyone, including us.
This is disqualifying under the data-and-code policy of every journal on the
target list.

**Action.** Restore or rebuild the nine parcel files (`attach_census.py`,
`attach_crime.py`, `attach_amenities.py`, `attach_micro_geo.py`,
`build_canonical_confounders.py`), then re-run the panel. `data/raw/` holds nothing
for those nine either, so this means re-downloading from the city portals via
`data/scripts/city_downloaders/`, not a local rebuild. No published number can be
regenerated until it is done.

This does **not** block the diagnostic work. The listing files carry `beds`,
`baths`, `sqft`, `year_built`, `property_type`, coordinates and price for all
twelve markets, which is enough to characterise the estimator's behaviour, just not
enough to reproduce the published estimand. Nor does it block the sale-price
extension, which needs a fetcher, not a parcel join.

Two smaller bugs surfaced while doing this, both of which crash the pipeline on
current data and both of which should be fixed in `causal_inference.py`:

- `zip` is cast with `emb_df["zip"].fillna(0).astype(float)`. Chicago has
  empty-string ZIPs and NYC has ZIP+4 (`11355-4788`). Both raise.
- `property_type` never enters the confounder set at all. See below.

## Substantive: the text axis is proxying property type, not semantics

`canonical_confounders.PROPERTY` is `[bedrooms, bldg_area_sqft, lot_area_sqft,
year_built]`. There is no property-type variable anywhere in the design, and the
nearest-parcel join cannot supply one, since for a vacant lot it returns a
neighbouring built parcel's attributes.

A cross-fitted logit of the vacant-land indicator confirms the design is blind to
it, and that the text is not:

| market | AUC(land ~ confounders) | AUC(land ~ text PC1) | land share |
|---|---|---|---|
| boston | 0.502 | 0.954 | 1.9% |
| sf | 0.674 | 0.913 | 3.5% |
| nyc | 0.468 | 0.544 | 1.2% |

Dropping the vacant-land listings, in the three markets where the full confounder
block exists:

| market | \|θ\| full | \|θ\| no land | attenuation | net of rescaling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| boston | 0.0733 | 0.0075 (CI covers 0) | 89.8% | 89.2% |
| sf | 0.1056 | 0.0606 | 42.7% | 39.5% |
| nyc | 0.0088 | 0.0073 | 16.9% | 17.0% |

Under the published confounder block, Boston's entire estimated text effect is
carried by 49 listings out of 2610. That number is spec-specific and should be
quoted as such: see the "how far this generalises" subsection below, where the
same drop under a listing-level confounder block costs Boston only 20%. The 49 are
33 vacant-land listings and 16 parking spaces, so the category is non-residential
listings, not vacant lots.

Three checks rule out the obvious alternatives.

*Rescaling.* `run_dml` restandardises the treatment on whatever subsample it
receives, so dropping tail observations shrinks a per-sigma coefficient
mechanically. Putting both estimates on the full-sample sigma scale accounts for
about five percentage points of the drop, not the drop.

*Leverage.* Dropping 49 **random** non-land Boston listings leaves θ at
0.0771 (sd 0.0028, 95% [0.0720, 0.0824]); the drop-land estimate sits at the 0th
percentile of that null. Dropping the 49 non-land listings **furthest out in the
same tail of T that land occupies** moves θ to 0.0982, i.e. *up* 34%. So it is
landness, not extremeness. San Francisco behaves identically (0.1408 on the tail
drop, 0.0606 on the land drop).

*Internal control.* NYC is the market where the text axis does not encode
property type (AUC 0.544) and it is the market where dropping land changes
nothing. The mechanism predicts exactly that.

Across all twelve markets, land share correlates with the **published** effects at
Pearson +0.90 (Shen) and +0.88 (Baur), leave-one-out stable in [+0.83, +0.93].
With Philadelphia and Chicago both removed it falls to +0.48 on ten points. That
comparison is weaker than it looks, because the published θ for nine of the twelve
markets came from a specification we cannot regenerate, so it should not be leaned
on. The properly-controlled version is below and it is more modest.

### How far this generalises: a cleaner test, and a correction

Dropping rows changes n, changes sd(T), and invites a leverage story. A better
test leaves the sample alone. Every market carries the listing's own `beds`,
`baths`, `sqft` and `year_built` (61--98% coverage) plus `property_type` at 100%,
none of which requires a parcel file. Build the confounder block from those, then
adjust for property type two ways and compare against no adjustment at all.
`data/scripts/property_type_confounding.py` does this for all twelve.

The first thing it shows is a bug in the fix this plan originally recommended.
**Adding `property_type` dummies to a RidgeCV nuisance does not adjust for property
type.** The penalty shrinks a rare dummy toward zero, so in Boston, where
non-residential listings are 1.9% of the sample, adding the dummies *raises* |θ|
from 0.397 to 0.446. Exact within-type demeaning of T and Y, which is unpenalised,
lowers it to 0.337. Use the demeaning, or unpenalised fixed effects; do not use a
penalised dummy.

With exact within-type demeaning, no rows dropped, listing-level controls:

| market | non-res share | \|θ\| | \|θ\| demeaned | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| philadelphia | 19.7% | 0.3044 | 0.1842 | −39.5% |
| chicago | 12.4% | 0.1818 | 0.1592 | −12.4% |
| phoenix | 4.6% | 0.0464 | 0.0347 | −25.1% |
| sf | 3.6% | 0.2153 | 0.1785 | −17.1% |
| boston | 1.9% | 0.3967 | 0.3374 | −15.0% |
| nyc | 1.1% | 0.1286 | 0.1294 | −0.6% |

Philadelphia, which the referee will ask about, is the market with the highest
non-residential share and the largest attenuation. New York, the lowest share, has
none. That pairing is the finding, and it holds without dropping a single row.

But the cross-market gradient is weak. Over all twelve, non-residential share
against the attenuation gives Pearson +0.63 and **Spearman only +0.27**; excluding
DC, whose |θ| of 0.020 makes a ratio meaningless, Pearson +0.67 and Spearman
+0.19. The Pearson is Philadelphia's leverage. Boston attenuates 15% on a 1.9%
share while Atlanta attenuates 7% on a 7.1% share.

**So the honest claim is narrower than the one this plan first made.** Property-type
composition demonstrably inflates Philadelphia's effect, is invisible in New York,
and moves most markets somewhere between five and twenty-five percent. It is not a
general explanation of the cross-market heterogeneity, and it is not established
that it explains the null moderator result. Anyone quoting the +0.90 correlation
should quote the Spearman +0.19 next to it.

**What this does and does not do to the paper.** It does not touch the central
claim, which is that the *geography* the representation encodes is not the
geography that moves price. It does destroy the interpretation layered on top of
it. The paper offers two readings, semantic content and laundered geography, and
concludes for the first because the second fails a spatial-adjustment test. There
is a third reading it never considers: the text encodes an unmodelled *structured*
attribute. Vacant land is the clearest instance and the one we can measure.
Condition, tenure and distress are the same phenomenon and are not in the
confounder set either.

The reframe is available. "Listing text prices property attributes the tabular data
omits" is a real, defensible, useful finding for an AVM audience. Whether it also
explains the cross-market heterogeneity that Section `sec:meta-12` searched for
demographic moderators of is an open question, not a settled one: the rank
correlation between non-residential share and attenuation is +0.19. An earlier
draft of this plan asserted "the moderator was composition all along." That is not
supported and has been withdrawn.

The New York exception, which the abstract and the conclusion both turn on, is the
weakest thing in the paper and needs re-examination on two independent grounds.
It does not reproduce: the committed estimate is `-0.0188` with an interval
excluding zero, the rerun is `+0.0088` with an interval covering zero. And
composition offers a competing explanation the paper never considers, since NYC
has both the lowest land share of the twelve (1.2%) and the smallest effect,
exactly as the mechanism above predicts. The paper instead attributes the null to
NYC's text being "the most geographic of the twelve." That may still be true, but
it is currently unidentified against composition, and the estimate it rests on
does not regenerate.

## Must rerun

In order. Nothing downstream is meaningful before step 1.

1. Restore the nine missing parcel files; fix the two `zip` casts. Verify the
   twelve committed `baur_pooled_pca/*.json` regenerate.
2. Adjust for `property_type` by exact within-type demeaning or unpenalised fixed
   effects, **not** by adding dummies to the ridge nuisance, which shrinks a rare
   category to no effect. Add missingness indicators for the structured columns and
   median-impute rather than zero-fill. Report the panel with and without
   non-residential listings.
3. Re-estimate the panel on the **out-of-fold** principal component, not the
   in-sample pooled one. Appendix F's own Table `tab:sim_coverage` shows the
   in-sample projection covering at 0.78 for n=500 at the largest simulated
   effect, and Chicago, Philadelphia and Atlanta all carry estimates larger than
   the largest effect that table simulates (estimand 0.231).
4. Report percentile-bootstrap intervals over the full pipeline, refitting the
   projection and both nuisance models inside each resample. No bootstrap
   interval is currently reported for any market, contrary to Appendix F.
5. Extend the simulation's η grid past 0.10 so it covers the effect sizes the
   panel actually reports.
6. Redo the Gelbach decomposition, the LEACE diagnostic, the sensitivity analysis
   and the meta-regression on the corrected sample. The τ² collapse under the
   land drop suggests the meta-regression null will not survive.

## Sale-price extension: 4 → 8 markets

The outcome is asking price for the headline and recorded sale price for four
markets in `sec:saleprice`. For JAE, REE or JUE the sale price should be the
headline: the agent sets price and prose jointly, and the paper concedes this.

Verified availability for the eight markets currently without recorded sales:

| market | verdict | source | difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| seattle | available | King County Assessor `EXTR_RPSale`, free bulk extract | easy |
| denver | available | Denver Open Data, fields `salePrice`/`saleDate`/`parcel_id`, 2008– | easy |
| phoenix | available | Maricopa County Assessor sales-affidavit bulk ZIP | easy |
| atlanta | partial | Fulton County qPublic per-parcel; GSCCCA PT-61 index is paid | scrape |
| boston | partial | MassLandRecords per-deed only; Boston assessing data has no sale field | hard |
| sf | proxy only | PCOR is confidential by CA statute; price must be backed out of documentary transfer tax | hard |
| portland | partial | Oregon has no transfer-tax affidavit post-Measure 79; price only in the deed image, paid remote access | hard |
| dallas | **impossible** | Texas is a statutory non-disclosure state, Tax Code §22.27, Gov. Code §552.149 | n/a |

Seattle, Denver and Phoenix are three free bulk downloads and take the panel from
four to seven recorded-price markets. Atlanta makes eight with a scraper. Dallas
cannot be done from public record at any price and should be stated as a
limitation rather than left unexplained.

## Venue

`drafts_jbes_2026/` presumes JBES. JBES asks for a methodological contribution,
and the paper explicitly disclaims one; its one methods result, the
generated-regressor undercoverage, is a confirmation of the prescription in
`egami2022texts`, which it cites for exactly that. Journal of Applied
Econometrics is the better target: its scope names "the application of existing
as well as new econometric techniques", `nowak2017textual` is a JAE paper,
`ahrens2025model` put DML in JAE last year, and the JAE data archive has a
standing confidential-data carve-out with a Nowak precedent for MLS-type data.
Real Estate Economics is the highest-probability acceptance (Gyger et al. 2025 is
a structural twin, spatial-ML hedonics with no causal design). Journal of Urban
Economics has the exact lineage through `shen2021information` but is a long shot.
Avoid RSUE, whose stated scope requires "a convincing identification strategy"
for any paper studying causal mechanisms.

Cosmetically: the paper is `\documentclass{article}`, `unsrtnat`, has a section
titled "Related Work", and is stamped "Preprint". No economics or statistics
journal is written that way.
