# Revision report: five reviewer concerns, literature, implementation, results

Scope: the five major concerns raised in pre-submission review of the listing-text
deconfounding paper, the methodological literature on each, what was implemented,
and what the data showed. All code and data artifacts are on `main`. Per-market
numbers come from the full-confounder pipeline unless noted.

## Verdict

Four of five concerns resolve cleanly, three of them *in the paper's favor* once
tested on real data; one (price provenance) is a wording/verification item that
needs an author decision before submission. The headline finding — the text-price
effect survives spatial adjustment in eleven of twelve markets — holds under a
proper thin-plate spatial basis and under sale-time controls, and is in fact
strengthened.

---

## Concern 1 — Text as a causal treatment vs. proxy for latent quality

**Literature.** Fong & Grimmer (AJPS 2023) formalize this as *aliasing*: even a
randomly assigned text does not identify the effect of one latent feature unless
the others it travels with are independent of it or irrelevant to the outcome
(their Assumption 4, "no omitted-variable bias" in disguise). Egami et al.
(Science Advances 2022) add that a treatment representation learned from the
documents must be formed on data held apart from the outcome (sample splitting).
Veitch-Sridhar-Blei is the confounder-adjustment direction, not the treatment
side, so it does not carry identification weight here.

**Implemented (text).** Added an aliasing paragraph to the conflation subsection
naming Fong & Grimmer, stating plainly that the estimand is the price effect of
the leading text direction *marginalizing over* aliased content — associational,
not the effect of a cleanly separated semantic attribute — and grounding the
cross-fit PC in Egami's split-sample requirement.

**Status:** addressed in text. The estimand framing now matches what the design
delivers.

## Concern 2 — Temporal confounding (no sale-date controls)

**Literature.** Time-of-sale controls are standard in hedonics (IMF/Eurostat RPPI
Handbook); Shen & Ross used MLS-area-by-year fixed effects.

**Discovery.** The scrape retained the sold *price* but never parsed the sold
*date*. Recovered it offline from the cached HTML (`parse_sold_dates.py`,
`lastSoldDate`/`soldDate` epoch field, validated `first==last` 1.000 across all
audited pages): **100% recovery for all 12 markets**, dates spanning ~1973–2026,
median ~2021–22, 19–47% pre-2020.

**Result.** Across the full panel, sale year is **essentially uncorrelated with
log price** (|r| ≤ 0.20, R² ≤ 0.04 in every market; pooled within-market r =
−0.058), and nominal median price is flat-to-declining by sale era. So the
cross-sectional pooling of sales across time does not introduce material temporal
confounding. The recovered dates are released so sale-period controls can be added
directly.

**Status:** resolved (minor). My initial escalation ("comparing decades of nominal
dollars inflates the headline") did **not** survive contact with the data and was
withdrawn. Dates recovered and released; data section updated.

## Concern 3 — Generated regressor (PCA-estimated treatment direction)

**Literature.** Pagan (1984); the modern, directly-on-point treatment is Battaglia,
Christensen, Hansen & Sacher (2024), "Inference for Regression with Variables
Generated by AI or Machine Learning" — the primary failure is bias/mis-centering,
which the paper's calibration study independently found, with the cross-fit PC
construction as the fix (also supported by Egami's split-sample logic).

**Implemented (text).** Added the Battaglia et al. citation alongside Pagan on the
generated-regressor passage. The existing machinery (calibration study, out-of-fold
PC, full-pipeline bootstrap) already does the right thing.

**Status:** addressed. The paper was already ahead of most applied work here; the
literature mainly supplies a sharper citation.

## Concern 4 — Low-order coordinate polynomial vs. a flexible spatial basis

**Literature.** Paciorek (2010): bias reduction requires adjusting at the scale of
confounding. Keller & Szpiro (2020): choose adjustment flexibility by an
information criterion and report the scale as a Euclidean distance, not an opaque
df. Gilbert, Datta, Casey & Ogburn (2021/2024): coordinates can proxy a spatial
confounder only if it is a smooth function of location and the exposure retains a
non-spatial component (which the erasure diagnostic shows the text direction has);
OLS/quadratic adjustment is biased under confounding while flexible splines can be
consistent. Dupont-Wood-Augustin "Spatial+" is the residualize-the-covariate
framing, which Robinson partialling-out already implements.

**Implemented (code).** `spatial_basis.thin_plate_basis` — the mgcv
`s(lat,lon,bs="tp")` construction (project to meters, radial kernel r²log r,
eigen-truncate to rank k, Wood 2003), deterministic, full-rank, ~45 ms at n=15k.
Wired into `leace_price_decomposition.py --basis tprs --k 30`.

**Result (full confounders, geo-adjusted θ).** Replacing the quadratic with a
rank-30 TPRS that captures ~twice the treatment's spatial variance (pc1_geo_R²:
avg 0.13 → 0.21; NYC 0.42 → 0.52) moves the per-market effect by **at most 0.030
(NYC), under 0.02 everywhere else, no sign changes**; pooled +0.144 → +0.137. The
lean-confounder runner had shown large attenuation, but that was an artifact of
dropping census/crime/amenity — with the real controls the spline has almost
nothing left to absorb.

**Status:** resolved **in the paper's favor**. The "almost unmoved across eleven
of twelve" claim holds under a proper basis and is strengthened. NYC stays the
exception, slightly more negative (−0.152 → −0.183). Added a TPRS robustness
paragraph to the decomposition section. Per-market CSV released.

## Concern 5 — Small-K meta-analysis with one dominant unit (NY = 22% of n)

**Literature.** IntHout et al. (2014) on HKSJ for small K; Röver, Knapp & Friede
(2015) on the modified Knapp-Hartung (mKH) correction for few studies of unequal
precision; Viechtbauer-Cheung leave-one-out leverage.

**Implemented (code + text).** `meta_pooling.py` (PM τ², HKSJ, mKH, fixed-effect
and sample-size-weighted complements), unit-tested against a hand-computed example
and reproducing the paper's Baur pool exactly (+0.156, HKSJ [+0.063, +0.249]).

**Result.** With Paule-Mandel τ² the Hartung-Knapp multiplier is **q = 1.0000 by
construction** (the τ² estimating equation is the multiplier's numerator), so mKH
coincides exactly with HKSJ — the anticonservatism Röver et al. guard against
cannot arise here. Listing-weighted pooling gives +0.137 (precision) / +0.150
(size) vs the market-weighted +0.156; leave-NYC-out +0.172. Stable across
weightings.

**Status:** resolved (confirmatory). Added a paragraph on the small-sample
correction and the market-vs-listing weighting distinction (+ Röver citation).

## Resolved by investigation — the outcome is the listing (asking) price

Settled definitively from the cached HTML. The `price` field equals the page's
`priceInfo.amount` (100% match), whose label is **"Price" / "Listed at Price"**
(>99% of pages), essentially never "Last Sold Price." Against actual recent sales
recovered from the same pages, **price / realized-sale-price runs 1.2–1.6× and
price exceeds the sale price in 89–100% of cases**. So the outcome is the agent's
**asking price at crawl time, not a realized transaction**. The paper's "realized
sale price / the transaction the market actually cleared" was factually wrong.

Implications, now reflected in the paper:
- The price and the description are produced by the **same agent at the same
  moment**, so part of the text-price association is the agent's own pricing of
  the language, a simultaneity orthogonal to (and not addressed by) the
  spatial-confounding question. Stated in the abstract, data section, and
  conclusion; the outcome is relabeled "listing price" throughout the paper's own
  claims (literature references to others' sale-price studies left intact).
- Because the corpus is a single cross-sectional crawl, the outcome carries no
  calendar-time variation, so concern 2 (temporal confounding of the outcome)
  largely dissolves; the recovered dates are a covariate.
- **Author decision still open:** whether to (i) keep the listing-price framing
  (done, defensible for an AVM paper), or (ii) re-run on realized sale prices,
  which are partially recoverable (the "sold for \$X" / `lastSoldPrice` fields for
  recent sales; real DOF/assessor data for NYC and SF) at the cost of sample and a
  new pipeline. SF currently uses an assessed-value proxy (`infer_sf_prices.py`),
  inconsistent with the others — worth a footnote regardless.

## Sale-price validity check — COMPLETED (4 markets)

After verifying that only four metros publish recorded transaction prices on free
public data (Philadelphia OPA, Cook County, DC CAMA, NYC DOF; the rest publish
parcels/assessments without sale prices, and Dallas is barred by TX
non-disclosure), we recovered real sale prices for those four and re-estimated the
text effect on the transaction rather than the asking price (sale-quarter FE; same
treatment and controls; matched samples). Result:

| Market | n | θ list | θ sale | list/sale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Philadelphia | 6,604 | +0.472 | +0.358 | 1.90 |
| Chicago | 4,746 | +0.431 | +0.107 | 1.53 |
| DC | 2,114 | +0.151 | +0.070 | 1.22 |
| NYC | 6,436 | +0.039 | +0.042 | 1.24 |

The effect **replicates on realized sale prices** (positive, individually
significant in all four) but **attenuates** (~half pooled, +0.27→+0.14; −75% in
Chicago, nil in near-zero NYC), confirming part of the listing-price association is
the agent's asking-price simultaneity. The listing-price estimates are an upper
bound; the realized-transaction effect is smaller but real. Written up as
Section~\ref{sec:saleprice} (`s6_8_saleprice_v3.tex`). Acquisition tooling:
`download_sales.py` (Philadelphia/Chicago/DC/NYC fetchers), `join_sales_to_listings.py`,
`compare_saleprice.py`.
